AI and robotization, dial by dial

Built on the task-based model of Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter and McCrory (2026), Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI (The Anthropic Institute's post and scenario explorer). The original model has one shock, AI automating cognitive occupations, and its results stop at 2030. This version adds a second shock, robotization of all other occupations, with the same dials, and extends the horizon to 2050.

The model runs monthly in your browser, so every chart re-runs as you move a dial. Values are gaps from an economy without AI or robotization unless the chart says otherwise.

How to read it. Solid lines are the current dials. After you pin a setting, it stays on every chart as dashed lines and in the table, so you can move the dials against it. Hover or drag across any chart to read every chart at that month. The grey line at 2030 marks where the paper's results stop; beyond it the paths follow the paper's equations unchanged.

What the model assumes. The labor force is fixed and there is no reservation wage, so workers stay in the market however low their wage. Unemployment comes only from frictions while one group sheds workers and the other absorbs them. When both groups are automated at the same pace, nobody is displaced, wages fall instead, and capital takes the income. There is no redistribution: income by source is before taxes and transfers.

Units. "Tasks it can do" is the affected mass of the paper, shown as a share of the group's own work. The gain per task is in log points (0.45 is a 57 percent unit-cost saving). Robotization starts from the AI anchors rescaled to the all-other group: 22 percent of tasks affected and 10 percent diffusion in mid-2026.
